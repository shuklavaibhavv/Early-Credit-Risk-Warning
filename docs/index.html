📊 Dual-Path Ratio Engine

Different industries need different solvency diagnostics. The engine auto-detects sector type and applies the correct ratio suite:

  • Non-Banks (Retail, Chemical, Tech): Altman Z-Score, Debt/EBITDA, Interest Coverage, Current Ratio, Cash Burn Rate
  • Banks: CET1 Capital Ratio, Loan-to-Deposit, NPL Ratio, Net Interest Margin, Bank Leverage

Each ratio is standardized before entering the composite model. Division-by-zero is handled with safe fallback logic.

🏛 Merton Distance-to-Default

Treats equity as a European call option on the firm's total asset value, with debt face value as the strike price:

The model iteratively solves for unobservable asset value (VA) and asset volatility (σA) using daily market data — giving real-time solvency signals that lead accounting data by weeks or months.

🎯 Key Research Finding

The Credit Suisse case study demonstrates why combining market-based and accounting-based signals matters:

  • CS's CET1 ratio reported 13.3% in Q2 2022 — well above FINMA's regulatory minimum — suggesting safety.
  • But Merton DD dropped to 1.74 that same quarter, with an implied default probability of 4.12%, flagging real trouble.
  • The accounting signal lagged the market signal by 2–3 quarters — exactly as predicted by Bharath & Shumway (2008).
  • Result: 3 quarters of advance warning before the March 2023 UBS rescue.

Across all 7 distressed firms, the model averaged 4.57 quarters (≈13.7 months) of early warning with a 0% false positive rate on 7 healthy controls.
